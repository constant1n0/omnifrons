## Decision

The public project name is **Omnifrons**. The descriptor is **“One context. Every model.”**

The coined name expresses one durable context and identity presenting different model/harness combinations as different faces. It is inspired by `omni`, `frons`, and the many-faced Janus Quadrifrons metaphor; this is product etymology, not a claim that `Omnifrons` is an attested classical Latin word.

## Preliminary screening

Exact-name searches performed on 2026-09-01 found no obvious collision in indexed web results, GitHub repositories, or common software package registries, and no indexed exact trademark result. The repository identifier `constant1n0/omnifrons` was available and selected.

This is a knockout screen only. It does not establish registrability, priority, ownership, freedom to operate, domain availability, or absence of confusingly similar marks. Official registry and professional legal searches remain pending.

Rejected finalists included `mementOS`, due to direct AI/software-memory uses, and `ContextOrbit`, due to overlapping software/product usage. The former working codename was rejected as crowded and descriptive.

## Formal clearance gate

Before a paid branded launch, permanent installer identity, or trademark filing, record:

1. target users, goods/services, channels, territories, and relevant classes;
2. professional exact and confusing-similarity searches;
3. reviewed OEPM, EUIPO, WIPO, and target-market registry results;
4. marketplace, company, open-source, app-store, package, domain, and unregistered-use checks;
5. pronunciation, accessibility, transliteration, case-insensitive filesystem, executable, and URL checks;
6. intended owner, filing/renewal plan, community-use rules, and enforcement responsibility;
7. at least one fallback and a tested rename path;
8. Legal Counsel advice and explicit Project Owner acceptance of residual risk.

Domain or repository availability is technical evidence, never trademark clearance.

## Interim public-name rules

- Do not claim registration, exclusivity, endorsement, or affiliation.
- Keep bundle, package, executable, protocol, and schema identifiers migratable until formal clearance.
- Do not use third-party logos or brand assets without permission.
- Use factual “works with” language only after compatibility is verified.
- Preserve a rename budget and migration plan before beta.

Names such as Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Git, Engram, Obsidian, OpenCode, and Proton Drive identify third-party products or projects. Their licenses do not automatically grant associated trademark rights.
